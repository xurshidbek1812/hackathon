Every traffic event on the road,
and a warning before the crash.

A fixed CCTV camera, 14 event classes, no labels. We detect and track every road user, turn trajectories into events with scene-aware rules, and score accident risk frame by frame using only the past.

Problem & approach

Part A returns every event as [start_sec, end_sec, label], matched at temporal IoU 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7. Part B returns, at every frame, the probability that an accident starts within 5 s — using only frames already seen.

1 · Detect & track

YOLO11-m (COCO, open weights) finds cars, buses, trucks, motorbikes, bicycles, people, animals and loose objects at 960 px on every 2nd frame. Riders and people inside vehicles are suppressed so they never look like pedestrians. Our own ByteTrack-style tracker (two-stage IoU matching, constant-velocity prediction, timestamps not frame counts) links boxes into tracks; fragments of a parked vehicle are re-joined after occlusion.

2 · Trajectories in body units

We take the bottom-centre of each box as its ground point, smooth it, and measure speed in body units per second (pixels ÷ √(w·h)). Far and near cars get comparable thresholds without camera calibration. A flow field learned from the sample videos gives each road cell its usual driving direction and marks where vehicles routinely queue.

3 · Rules → segments

Each class is a small, testable rule on trajectories plus the scene layout (stop lines, crossings, solid lines, signal-head position, allowed turns) from camera.md. Frame flags become segments; gaps are bridged, blips dropped and same-class overlaps merged, because boundaries are scored at IoU 0.7.

Technical report

What we built

A single-pass pipeline: YOLO11 detection on every second frame, our own ByteTrack-style tracker, smoothed trajectories measured in body units, and one small rule per event class that combines trajectories with the fixed scene layout. Classes a COCO detector cannot see get pixel cues: signal state from the signal-head region, static foreground blobs against a median background (debris), and flickering fire-coloured regions (fire), grey growing low-texture regions (smoke). Part B runs its own causal tracker and combines time-to-collision, hard braking, swerving, wrong-way motion and pedestrians on the road into a logistic risk score with fast rise and slow decay.

What worked

  • Body-unit speeds. Dividing pixel motion by box size made one set of thresholds work across the depth of the image.
  • Learned flow field. Lane directions and queue areas come from the data itself, so wrong-way and stopped-vehicle rules work even where the layout is hard to draw by hand.
  • Depth check for contact. Overlapping boxes only count as contact when their ground points are at a similar depth — this removes most perspective overlaps.
  • Conservative rare classes. A class we predict but that never occurs costs a full class of macro-F1, so rare classes need strong evidence.

What did not work (yet)

  • Accidents and near misses from rules alone are brittle: occlusion and ID switches at the moment of impact break trajectories.
  • Smoke from colour and texture alone confuses fog, dust and exhaust; we keep it very conservative.
  • Image-plane time-to-collision ignores depth; vehicles in different lanes can look like they are converging.

What we would do next

  • Fine-tune a video classifier (e.g. VideoMAE) on CCD / DoTA to re-score accident and near-miss candidates.
  • Estimate a ground-plane homography from lane markings to get metric speeds and true time-to-collision.
  • Fit the Part B weights with logistic regression on our own dev labels instead of hand-set weights.

Reproducibility

Fixed seeds, deterministic tracker and rules, no internet at inference, weights in the repository. Frame skipping only kicks in as an emergency if a machine runs at more than twice the time budget.
